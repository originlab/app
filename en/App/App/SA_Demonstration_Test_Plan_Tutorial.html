2.5.10.1 Tutorial for Demonstration Test Plan

Tutorial-Demonostration-Test-Plan

The Demonstration Test Plan is a pre-test design tool for reliability validation. It translates a reliability target, a confidence level, and engineering assumptions into an actionable sampling rule:

"test this many units for this long, allow this many failures, and if the result passes, you may claim the target is met at the stated confidence."

Topics for Demonstration:

  • Algorithm


In this example, a Electric Vehicle factory enhanced the lifetime of their high-voltage battery, in which failure is defined as the battery capacity drops below 80%. Before mass production, engineers needs to design a substantiantion arming at a claim that the improved battery pack (Gen-2) is at least as durable as the original pack (Gen-1).

Engineers collected the neccessary historical data from Gen-1:

  • battery life time follows a Weibull distribution.
  • Shape parameter = 2.8 (>1, a wear-out failure mode).
  • Scale parameter = 2400 equivalent full charge-discharge cycles (characteristic life), i.e., ~63.2% batteries lose more than 20% capacity before 2400 cycles.

Engineers sampled 15 battery pack from the prototypes of Gen-2 and use Demonstration to create a test plan that validates the characteristic life of Gen-2 is not less than that of Gen-1.

Steps

  1. Select menu Statistics: Survival Analysis: Reliability and Survival Analysis. In the opened panel, click Life Test Plans block and then choose Demonstration icon.
  2. In the dialog that opens, specify the settings on the Input tab as following :
    Calculate Determines which quantity to be calculated, the test duration per unit or the sample sizes.
    Minimum Value to be Demonstrated Specify the reliability target. Choose the statement that matches your spectification.
    • Scale (Weibull or Exponential) or Location (other distributions):
      Demonstrate that the distribution's Scale parameter (characteristic life) for Weibull or exponential distribution or Location parameter for normal, lognormal, logistic, etc. distributions is at least the specified Scale or Location value.
    • Percentile and Percent:
      Demonstrate that no more than the Percent of units have failed by the Percentile time.
    • Reliability and Time:
      Demonstrate that at least the Reliability (survival probability) of units still run at the Time. It is mathematically equivalent to the Percentile and Percent option but may match your specification language better.
    • MTTF (mean time to fail):
      Demonstrate that the MTTF is at least the entered value. It is usually used in an Exponential distribution, where MTTF equals the scale parameter and is the natural metric.
    Maximum Number of Failures Allowed Specify the largest number of failures that still allows the test to be declared a pass.

    0 states the strictest rule, i.e., any observed failure causes the demonstration to fail. It yields the largest required sample size or test time but provides the cleanest pass/fail statement.
    Entering 1, 2, ... states a more tolerant rules. They reduce the required sample size or test time but make the test less decisive and may provide some failure-time data for further analysis.

    Note: You may enter multiple values separated by space (or a column of values) to compare how relaxing the acceptance rule affects the plan.

    Sample Sizes / Testing Times Specify the sample sizes when Calculate = Time, or test durations for each unit if Calculate = Size. Increasing sample size shortens the required test time per unit. vice versa.

    Note: You may enter multiple values separated by space (or a column of values) to compare sample size against test duration.

    Distribution Select the probability distribution and the assumed Scale/Shape parameter value used to model failure times.

    The test plan can be sensitive to the specified Shape (Weibull) or Scale (other distributions) so it is good to enter a range of plausible values and compare the results.

  3. On the Plots tab, leave the default settings untouched. Refer to "Results and Interpretation" section for the explanation of Probability of passing the demonstration test.
  4. Click OK button to generate report.

Results and Interpretation

Test Plans

  • Take 15 Gen-2 battery packs and test each one to 1351 equivalent full cycles.
  • If 0 packs fail before 1351 cycles: the demonstration passes. You may state "at 95% confidence that the Gen-2 meets or exceeds the Gen-1 characteristic life - 2400 cycles.
  • If 1 or more packs fail before 1351 cycles: the demonstration does not pass. You cannot make the 95% confidence claim using this plan. This does NOT prove Gen-2 is worse; it only means the evidence is insufficient under the chosen (sample size, test time, and maximum number of failures allowed) scheme.

Likelihood of Passing Plot

  • The curve shows how likely this plan is to pass if the true Gen-2 characteristic life is k times the Gen-1 baseline.
  • k = 1.0: If the true characteristic lifetime of Gen-2 is exactly 2400 cycles, the plan has only a 5% chance of passing — the specified lower confidence bound.
  • k = 2.0: If the true Gen-2 characteristic lifetime is twice as Gen-1, the plan has about a 65% chance of passing - roughly one in three such tests would fail despite the product being truly better.
    If the true improvement is about twice as durable and the team cannot accept a ~35% chance of failing the demonstration, allowing failures, e.g., Maximum number of failures allowed = 1 or 2, must be considered. Note that allowing a failure typically lengthens the per-unit test time.
  • k ≥ 4.0: The plan is very likely (>94%) to pass.

Conclusion

  • Run 15 Gen-2 battery packs to 1351 equivalent full cycles; with zero failures you can claim 95% confidence that the characteristic life is larger than 2400 cycles.
  • A test of 1351 cycles for a battery pack is very long (several years). Accelerated aging methods, such as high temperatures or charge-discharge rates are typically used to compress the testing period into just a few months.
